Critical Evaluation of Studies Alleging Evidence for Technosignatures in the POSS1-E Photographic Plates

Wesley Andrés Watters, Laura Dominé, Sarah Little, Cameron Pratt, Kevin H. Knuth & Matthew Szenher · Publications of the Astronomical Society of Australia · Published online July 23, 2026 · DOI 10.1017/pasa.2026.10230

Peer-reviewed methodological critique examining claims derived from pre-Sputnik Palomar Observatory Sky Survey photographic-plate features. For the datasets examined, the authors report no statistically significant Earth-shadow deficit and find that the reported nuclear-test timing correlation becomes insignificant after normalization by the relevant number of observation days; they also identify spatial non-uniformity, dataset inconsistencies, catalogue-star contamination, scan artifacts and plate defects as important limitations.

Methodology: The authors test assumptions used in the Earth-shadow analysis, evaluate reported linear feature clusters, inspect spatial distributions, and re-normalize the nuclear-test timing analysis using telescope observation days rather than the broader calendar interval.

Research context and limitations: The paper evaluates previously published datasets closely related to those used in the VASCO studies and does not independently establish the nature of every plate feature. VASCO researchers have disputed aspects of the critique and report separate replication or validation analyses, so GreyAlien preserves this as an active scientific dispute rather than a conclusive “debunking.”

The Civilizational Risks of a UAP Technology Arms Race: Nonhuman Technology and Global Security

Marik von Rennenkampff · The Sol Foundation · Vol. 2, No. 3 · August 2026

Policy paper examining the global-security risks that could arise if states were competing to exploit advanced technology of alleged nonhuman origin. It considers arms-race dynamics, secrecy, deterrence and international control while treating the existence or possession of such technology as a premise for policy analysis rather than an established GreyAlien fact.
