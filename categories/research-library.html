Notice of Intent to Sole Source: Historical UAP Data and Analysis Subscription

Washington Headquarters Services / AARO · Notice NUFOHRC_20260731 · Posted July 31, 2026

The notice describes an intended sole-source, firm-fixed-price commercial subscription providing AARO digital access to proprietary pre-1990 historical UAP data, metadata and analytical products associated with NUFOHRC. It describes a one-year base period with four possible one-year options and expressly distinguishes the subscription from professional labor or advisory services.

Status: This is a notice of intent, not evidence that GreyAlien has verified a completed award. Subscription access would not transfer ownership of the physical archive.

Historical significance: The proposed access connects AARO’s government historical review with major civilian collections reportedly preserved outside federal repositories, while remaining distinct from NARA’s Record Group 615.

Evidence limitation: Preservation and government access do not authenticate every report or conclusion contained in the archive.

Research / Scientific Preprint · Permanent

Limits on Velocity Recovery from the PURSUE Sensor Videos

Jacob Haqq-Misra & Ravi Kopparapu · arXiv:2608.12445v1 · Submitted August 12, 2026 · Preprint / peer review not established

The authors reviewed all 112 released PURSUE airborne sensor videos and found that none provides the complete range, aircraft-motion, viewing-geometry and sensor-scale information needed to recover physical velocity. The result is a limit on what the public data can establish—not an identification of the objects and not evidence for or against anomalous craft.

PR113: A visible angular graticule constrains camera scale, but missing range and platform data allow radically different size and velocity scenarios. The paper's Mach 5 example illustrates this degeneracy; it is not a claim that the video shows a Mach 5 object.

PR149: A vessel of known size provides an in-frame reference, supporting an upper bound near Mach 0.4 on relative velocity while leaving the object's exact speed and identity unresolved.

Research implication: Unredacted telemetry, flight logs, camera metadata, range histories or correlated radar tracks are needed before claims of anomalous physical speed can be evaluated reliably.

Research / Peer-Reviewed Accepted Manuscript · Permanent

Critical Evaluation of Studies Alleging Evidence for Technosignatures in the POSS1-E Photographic Plates

Wesley Andrés Watters, Laura Dominé, Sarah Little, Cameron Pratt, Kevin H. Knuth & Matthew Szenher · Publications of the Astronomical Society of Australia · Published online July 23, 2026 · DOI 10.1017/pasa.2026.10230

Peer-reviewed methodological critique examining claims derived from pre-Sputnik Palomar Observatory Sky Survey photographic-plate features. For the datasets examined, the authors report no statistically significant Earth-shadow deficit and find that the reported nuclear-test timing correlation becomes insignificant after normalization by the relevant number of observation days; they also identify spatial non-uniformity, dataset inconsistencies, catalogue-star contamination, scan artifacts and plate defects as important limitations.

Methodology: The authors test assumptions used in the Earth-shadow analysis, evaluate reported linear feature clusters, inspect spatial distributions, and re-normalize the nuclear-test timing analysis using telescope observation days rather than the broader calendar interval.

Research context and limitations: The paper evaluates previously published datasets closely related to those used in the VASCO studies and does not independently establish the nature of every plate feature. VASCO researchers have disputed aspects of the critique and report separate replication or validation analyses, so GreyAlien preserves this as an active scientific dispute rather than a conclusive “debunking.”

The Civilizational Risks of a UAP Technology Arms Race: Nonhuman Technology and Global Security

Marik von Rennenkampff · The Sol Foundation · Vol. 2, No. 3 · August 2026

Policy paper examining the global-security risks that could arise if states were competing to exploit advanced technology of alleged nonhuman origin. It considers arms-race dynamics, secrecy, deterrence and international control while treating the existence or possession of such technology as a premise for policy analysis rather than an established GreyAlien fact.
