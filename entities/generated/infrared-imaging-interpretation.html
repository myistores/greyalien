Infrared Imaging & Interpretation

Infrared recordings measure radiation outside visible light. Interpreting apparent brightness, shape and motion requires sensor context, calibration and geometry.

Established Science

Beyond visible light

Infrared is electromagnetic radiation at longer wavelengths than visible light. Near-infrared observations can record reflected light, while thermal bands allow observation of emitted radiation.

Established Science

Display versus object

An infrared picture represents detected radiation through an instrument and display. A bright feature alone does not establish its identity or a calibrated temperature.

Established Science

Reading a recording

Analysts need the spectral band, calibration, timing, image scale and processing history. Avoid assuming every military video is infrared.

Analytical Interpretation

Testing brightness changes

The PR135 authors compare periodic brightness changes with animal recordings and test planted constant-brightness controls. Their bird interpretation remains provisional.

Unknown

What remains unresolved?

A resemblance or shared frequency does not identify a species. Missing range and platform telemetry also limit physical-motion estimates.
