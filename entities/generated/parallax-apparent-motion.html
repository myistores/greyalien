Natural & Human-Made Explanations

Parallax & Apparent Motion

Observer movement and viewing geometry can make a nearby slow object appear to move rapidly against a distant background. Physical motion requires range, timing and platform context.

Established Science

Relative viewpoint

From a moving vehicle, nearby objects appear to shift against distant scenery. The relative displacement depends on geometry and distance; apparent screen motion is not a direct speedometer.

Established Science

What measurements are needed?

Recovering motion requires known timing, angular scale, range and observer motion. Different combinations can produce similar image tracks.

Analytical Interpretation

Applied UAP research

The PURSUE kinematic study illustrates ambiguity in PR113 and a constrained relative-speed estimate in PR149. Its conclusions concern the public recordings analyzed, rather than all UAP evidence.

Hypothesis

Birds and PR135

A separate preprint proposes a nearer flock as an explanation for PR135 using brightness measurements and geometry. Missing telemetry leaves this interpretation open to testing.

Unknown

Limits

Parallax is a geometric effect, not an object identity. Demonstrating a possible geometry does not prove that it occurred in a particular case.
