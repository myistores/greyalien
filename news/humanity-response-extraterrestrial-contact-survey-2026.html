How Would Humanity React to Alien Contact? International Survey Reveals Excitement and Concern

GreyAlien analysis of research by Michael Bohlander, Andreas Anton and John Elliott · Global Policy Journal · November 17, 2026

How might people respond if humanity received a signal from extraterrestrial intelligence? Researchers Michael Bohlander, Andreas Anton and John Elliott examined this question in a multilingual 2024 survey of approximately 6,500 valid responses. Their overview was published by Global Policy Journal on November 17, 2026.

Excitement, concern and uncertainty

Roughly three-quarters of respondents selected excitement as a reaction to a signal from space, while 23.9% selected concern. Because the survey permitted multiple answers, the categories could overlap. In another hypothetical scenario involving an alien craft landing on Earth, about 81–82% supported observing or attempting communication, while 13.4% favored attempting to destroy it.

Technology, human rights and governance

The researchers also asked respondents to consider whether humanity should accept human-rights violations as conditions for receiving potentially planet-saving extraterrestrial technology. Most such concessions received low support, but roughly one-quarter were willing to compromise freedom of religion or safeguards against intrusive criminal investigation; 6.2% favored surrendering humans for nonconsensual experimentation. These are hypothetical survey responses, not evidence of any actual offer or contact.

What the results cannot tell us

The sample was not representative of the world population. Germany, France, the United States and the United Kingdom contributed 81.6% of responses, and 75.2% of respondents were male. The survey measures the views of its participants and cannot establish what humanity as a whole would do. Respondents' beliefs that extraterrestrials have visited Earth are opinions, not independently verified evidence of visitation.

Why it matters to UAP and SETI research

Post-detection planning involves communication, social trust, scientific confirmation, ethics and governance. This study provides a starting point for discussing those questions, but it does not demonstrate extraterrestrial intelligence or predict actual behavior in a real contact event.
