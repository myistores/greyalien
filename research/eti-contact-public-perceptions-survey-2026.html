Human Life after Contact with Extraterrestrial Intelligence — 2024 International Survey

Michael Bohlander, Andreas Anton and John Elliott · Global Policy Journal summary, November 17, 2026

Research description

Multilingual 2024 survey (English, French, German and Spanish) of roughly 6,500 valid responses from 88 countries, exploring hypothetical reactions to extraterrestrial intelligence, expectations of contact, and the ethics of technological exchange.

Selected findings

  • Roughly three-quarters excited by a signal from space; 23.9% worried (overlap possible).
  • 59.5% believed ETI had previously visited Earth, a measure of belief rather than evidence.
  • 81–82% supported waiting/observing or attempting a welcome and communication after a hypothetical craft landing.
  • 6.2% accepted nonconsensual human experimentation in a hypothetical technology-exchange scenario.

Limitations and evidence status

Nonrepresentative voluntary-response sample: 81.6% of respondents were from Germany, France, the United States and United Kingdom; 75.2% were male. Findings should not be generalized to all humanity. The study measures attitudes, not observed reactions to actual contact or evidence of extraterrestrial visitation.
